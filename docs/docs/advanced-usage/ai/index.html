On-device AI

Where Chrome ships its built-in language model (Gemma Nano), Tabverse uses it for two small jobs, entirely on this device:

  • Summarize a tab into a new note - the page's visible text becomes a short, factual summary in a note attached to the current tabverse.
  • Suggest a name for the current tab collection, from its tabs' titles and hosts.

Where to find it​

Settings ▸ AI reports three things: whether this device can use the model at all, whether it is switched on (Use the on-device model), and whether the local model is installed. Chrome downloads the model once, on first use.

The two features are a lightbulb in the manager:

  • Name a tabverse - the lightbulb next to the tabverse's name at the top of the tab list. It suggests three names to pick from.
  • Summarize a tab - the lightbulb on the right of a live tab row. It turns that page into a new note in the Note tool.

Without Chrome's built-in model the two lightbulbs simply do not appear, and Settings ▸ AI says why.

AI history​

The AI history panel (the chat icon on the right rail) is a local log of what this extension asked the model: each entry shows the prompt and the answer, newest first, with a Clear button. It is kept on this device and is never synced.

Privacy​

Nothing leaves the browser: the text is handed to the model inside the page, and no request is made to us or to anyone else. The model itself is Chrome's own and is downloaded once by Chrome; it is not part of the extension.
